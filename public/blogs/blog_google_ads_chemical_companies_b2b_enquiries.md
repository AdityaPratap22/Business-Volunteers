## 2. Campaign Architecture: High-Intent Keywords vs. Negative Keywords

The primary reason many chemical companies waste ad spend on Google PPC is running broad, un-targeted campaigns that attract student researchers, job seekers, small retail buyers, or dangerous DIY searchers rather than B2B procurement decision-makers.

```
Chemical Google Ads Keyword Architecture:
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. HIGH-INTENT COMMERCIAL KEYWORDS (Target Exact & Phrase Match)               |
|  "specialty chemical manufacturers in Gurgaon", "bulk acrylic polymer supplier"  |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                       │
                                       ▼
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  2. NEGATIVE KEYWORDS (Exclude Non-Converting Traffic)                           |
|  -jobs, -recipe, -diy, -experiment, -home use, -salary, -retail, -hazardous     |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 3 Keyword Selection Rules for Chemical PPC
1. **Target Exact & Phrase Match Terms:** Focus ad budget on exact-match (e.g., `[acrylic polymer manufacturer in Gurgaon]`) and phrase-match (e.g., `"industrial solvent supplier"`) keywords to prevent irrelevant ad triggers.
2. **Eliminate Non-Buying Intent:** Maintain an extensive negative keyword list excluding terms like *jobs, recipe, diy, experiment, home use, salary, retail, equipment, and hazardous handling*.
3. **Target Specific Industrial Geographic Zones:** Limit ad delivery to major industrial hubs, manufacturing belts, or target export countries.

Broad match triggers ads for loosely related queries (including job searches, student homework, and DIY recipes), wasting ad budget. Exact match (`[keyword]`) triggers ads only when searchers type the precise technical commercial keyword, ensuring high lead quality.
