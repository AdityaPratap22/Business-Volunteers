## 2. Campaign Architecture: High-Intent Keywords vs. Negative Keywords

The primary reason many catering companies waste ad spend on Google PPC is running broad, un-targeted campaigns that attract job seekers, free recipe searchers, or small home order buyers rather than event decision-makers.

```
Catering Google Ads Keyword Architecture:
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. HIGH-INTENT COMMERCIAL KEYWORDS (Target Exact & Phrase Match)               |
|  "wedding catering services in Gurgaon", "corporate lunch caterers Cyber City"   |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                       │
                                       ▼
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  2. NEGATIVE KEYWORDS (Exclude Non-Converting Traffic)                           |
|  -jobs, -recipe, -cooking class, -franchise cost, -equipment, -salary, -cheap   |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 3 Keyword Selection Rules for Catering PPC
1. **Target Exact & Phrase Match Terms:** Focus ad budget on exact-match (e.g., `[wedding caterers in Gurgaon]`) and phrase-match (e.g., `"corporate lunch catering"`) keywords to prevent irrelevant ad triggers.
2. **Eliminate Non-Buying Intent:** Maintain an extensive negative keyword list excluding terms like *jobs, recipe, cooking class, equipment, supplier, franchise cost, salary, and cheap*.
3. **Hyper-Local City Targeting:** Limit ad delivery to specific cities and commercial districts where your catering team can realistically execute events.

### What is the difference between broad match and exact match keywords in catering PPC?
Broad match triggers ads for loosely related queries (including job searches and recipe recipes), wasting ad budget. Exact match (`[keyword]`) triggers ads only when searchers type the precise commercial keyword, ensuring high lead quality.

Google uses landing page speed and user experience to calculate Quality Scores. A sub-2 second landing page improves Quality Scores, lowers Cost-Per-Click (CPC), and prevents busy event planners from bouncing off.
